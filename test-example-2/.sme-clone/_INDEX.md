# Knowledge Index — Warren Buffett / Berkshire Hathaway Chairman's Letters (1977–2024)

This index catalogs 48 years of annual shareholder letters written by Warren E. Buffett as Chairman of Berkshire Hathaway Inc. Each letter is addressed directly to shareholders and covers annual performance, business philosophy, and capital allocation decisions. Use this index to locate source documents for specific years, topics, or recurring themes.

---

## Document Catalog

| Document | Year | Format | Summary |
| :--- | :---: | :--- | :--- |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1977.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201977.md) | 1977 | MHTML | First letter in the corpus. Covers 19% return on equity, insurance group growth (National Indemnity leading), textile division underperformance, and early articulation of the equity investment philosophy: buy understandable businesses with favorable long-term prospects at attractive prices. Introduces the Washington Post, GEICO, and Capital Cities positions. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1978.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201978.md) | 1978 | MHTML | Reports on sustained insurance underwriting profitability, continued difficulty in textiles, and growth of the equity portfolio. Reinforces the "hold for many years" stock philosophy. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1979.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201979.md) | 1979 | MHTML | Discusses inflation's impact on corporate returns, the declining utility of book value as a performance metric under inflation, and early articulation of the concept of economic goodwill vs. accounting goodwill. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1980.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201980.md) | 1980 | MHTML | Covers record insurance results. Introduces the "float" concept explicitly. Discusses the folly of confusing activity with accomplishment in business management. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1981.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201981.md) | 1981 | MHTML | Introduces the concept of "owner earnings" as superior to GAAP earnings. Discusses the economics of See's Candies as a prototype of an excellent business (high returns on capital, pricing power, low incremental capital requirements). |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1982.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201982.md) | 1982 | MHTML | Covers the acquisition of Nebraska Furniture Mart and Mrs. B (Rose Blumkin). Discusses how competitive advantages from brand and customer loyalty differ from those from patents or regulation. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1983.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201983.md) | 1983 | MHTML | The Blue Chip Stamps merger into Berkshire. Long discussion of goodwill accounting and why Berkshire treats economic goodwill differently from GAAP requirements. Acquisition of the Buffalo News. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1984.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201984.md) | 1984 | MHTML | Discusses the "drag of mushrooming capital base" on returns. Detailed analysis of stock repurchases as shareholder value creation. Explains insurance loss reserving errors. Nebraska Furniture Mart growth story. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1985.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201985.md) | 1985 | MHTML | Covers closing of Berkshire's textile mills. Candid post-mortem: kept the mills too long out of loyalty, acknowledges the error. Insurance business delivering exceptional float. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1986.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201986.md) | 1986 | MHTML | Discusses the difference between a "franchise" and a "commodity" business. Explains why Berkshire avoids hostile takeovers. Critiques LBO-driven acquisitions. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1987.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201987.md) | 1987 | MHTML | Written post-1987 stock market crash. Explains why Berkshire doesn't predict markets. Details on the investment in Salomon Brothers preferred. Discusses junk bonds and the risks of financial leverage. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1988.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201988.md) | 1988 | MHTML | Major new position in Coca-Cola announced. Explains the rationale: durable competitive advantage, global brand, high returns on capital, pricing power. Begins the "wonderful company at fair price" framing. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1989.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201989.md) | 1988 | MHTML | Covers the Gillette preferred investment. Introduces the concept of looking at retained earnings of investees ("look-through earnings"). Discusses the error of paying too much for acquisitions. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1990.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201990.md) | 1990 | MHTML | Salomon Brothers crisis begins. Insurance underwriting losses. Discusses the risks of financial leverage and why Berkshire maintains permanent financial strength. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1991.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201991.md) | 1991 | MHTML | Buffett becomes Interim Chairman of Salomon. Discusses the franchise vs. business distinction for media companies. Twenty-year anniversary of See's Candies acquisition — detailed case study on what makes an exceptional business. H.H. Brown shoe acquisition. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1992.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201992.md) | 1992 | MHTML | Introduces the "intrinsic value" concept formally. Discusses why Berkshire thinks in decades, not quarters. Dexter Shoe acquisition (later acknowledged as a mistake). |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1993.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201993.md) | 1993 | MHTML | Covers the Scott Fetzer businesses. Discusses incentive compensation philosophy: pay for performance on capital employed, not absolute earnings. Critiques options as compensation. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1994.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201994.md) | 1994 | MHTML | Discusses the economics of the insurance float as Berkshire's key competitive advantage. Details on GEICO's low-cost advantage in auto insurance. Explains why Berkshire doesn't forecast earnings. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1995.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201995.md) | 1995 | MHTML | GEICO acquisition (100%) announced. Explains why Berkshire held GEICO for 20 years before buying the rest. Discussion of competitive moats in insurance. |
| [Chairman's Letter - 1996.md](parsed/docs/Chairman's%20Letter%20-%201996.md) | 1996 | MHTML | Post-GEICO-acquisition letter. Discusses how GEICO's cost advantage is a structural competitive moat. Introduces Class B shares. Critiques financial engineering in acquisitions. |
| [1997 Chairman's Letter.md](parsed/docs/1997%20Chairman's%20Letter.md) | 1997 | MHTML | FlightSafety acquisition. Discusses "businesses with economic characteristics that allow owners to sit on their hands." Expands on durable competitive advantages. |
| [1998pdf.md](parsed/docs/1998pdf.md) | 1998 | PDF | General Re acquisition (large reinsurer). Later acknowledged as a mistake due to poor culture and derivatives exposure. Also covers the Executive Jet (NetJets) acquisition. |
| [final1999pdf.md](parsed/docs/final1999pdf.md) | 1999 | PDF | Berkshire's worst relative year vs. S&P 500 (up 0.5% vs. S&P +21%). Buffett explains why he didn't buy tech stocks, discusses the "franchise value" concept of American business, and warns about excessive market valuations. |
| [2000pdf.md](parsed/docs/2000pdf.md) | 2000 | PDF | Dot-com bust begins. Discusses the importance of buying businesses with durable economic characteristics. Midwestern businesses (MidAmerican Energy acquisition). Discusses float growing to $28 billion. |
| [2001pdf.md](parsed/docs/2001pdf.md) | 2001 | PDF | September 11 aftermath. Insurance losses ($2.4B from 9/11). Discusses how Berkshire's financial strength allowed it to pay claims immediately while others struggled. Derivatives called "financial weapons of mass destruction" for the first time. |
| [2002pdf.md](parsed/docs/2002pdf.md) | 2002 | PDF | Formal declaration of derivatives as dangerous. Detailed explanation of why General Re's derivatives book was impossible to unwind (took 5 years and $400M in losses). Corporate governance failures discussed. |
| [2003ltr.md](parsed/docs/2003ltr.md) | 2003 | PDF | Discusses the dollar weakness and global currency positions. Clayton Homes acquisition (manufactured housing). Discusses the importance of honest accounting vs. "earnings management." |
| [2004ltr.md](parsed/docs/2004ltr.md) | 2004 | PDF | Long discussion of the insurance business model and float. Critiques Wall Street fees and financial intermediary costs. Discusses how Berkshire's decentralized management works. |
| [2005ltr.md](parsed/docs/2005ltr.md) | 2005 | PDF | Hurricane Katrina losses. Discusses catastrophe insurance pricing. Introduces the concept of "intrinsic value" as the present value of future owner earnings. ISCAR acquisition (Israeli industrial tools). |
| [2006ltr.md](parsed/docs/2006ltr.md) | 2006 | PDF | Discusses the "Noah principle": predicting rain doesn't count, building arks does. Management hiring philosophy. PacifiCorp (utility) acquisition. |
| [2007ltr.md](parsed/docs/2007ltr.md) | 2007 | PDF | Pre-financial-crisis letter. Discusses the housing bubble and subprime mortgage risks. Clayton Homes' responsible lending practices vs. industry recklessness. Critiques ratings agencies. |
| [2008ltr.md](parsed/docs/2008ltr.md) | 2008 | PDF | Financial crisis letter. Famous "buy American, I am" framing (though Buffett already wrote it in NYT op-ed). Discusses Berkshire's four operating segments. Admits ConocoPhillips mistake. Bond insurance (BHAC) operations. Deep discussion of why derivatives are dangerous. |
| [2009ltr.md](parsed/docs/2009ltr.md) | 2009 | PDF | Burlington Northern Santa Fe (BNSF) acquisition — Berkshire's largest ever at the time ($26B). Called "an all-in wager on the economic future of the United States." Discusses why railroads have durable competitive advantages. |
| [2010ltr.md](parsed/docs/2010ltr.md) | 2010 | PDF | Post-crisis recovery. Discusses Berkshire's "four pillars": insurance, BNSF, MidAmerican Energy, and manufacturing/retail/services. Introduces the two-column framework for measuring Berkshire's value. |
| [2011ltr.md](parsed/docs/2011ltr.md) | 2011 | PDF | Discusses successor planning for the first time explicitly. Introduces the concept of "the test of time" for business quality. Stock buyback policy announced. |
| [2012ltr.md](parsed/docs/2012ltr.md) | 2012 | PDF | 50th anniversary of Buffett's involvement at Berkshire. Discusses the importance of culture in large organizations. Heinz acquisition partnership with 3G Capital discussed. |
| [2013ltr.md](parsed/docs/2013ltr.md) | 2013 | PDF | Berkshire's 50th anniversary letter. Two-part essay: Buffett on Berkshire's past, Charlie Munger on Berkshire's advantages. Discusses why Berkshire will never pay a dividend (the "Aesop" test). |
| [2014ltr.md](parsed/docs/2014ltr.md) | 2014 | PDF | "The American Tailwind" concept introduced. Discusses long-term optimism about America. Precision Castparts acquisition discussed (later completed 2016). Discusses why Berkshire avoids leverage. |
| [2015ltr.md](parsed/docs/2015ltr.md) | 2015 | PDF | Precision Castparts acquired ($32B, largest ever). Discusses why "EBITDA" is a misleading metric. Berkshire's decentralized operating model explained. |
| [2016ltr.md](parsed/docs/2016ltr.md) | 2016 | PDF | Discusses the power of passive index investing vs. active management. "The Bet" with hedge fund managers on S&P 500 performance. Apple stake begins (though not yet disclosed as large). |
| [2017ltr.md](parsed/docs/2017ltr.md) | 2017 | PDF | Tax Cuts and Jobs Act impact on Berkshire's deferred tax liabilities. Discusses why Berkshire holds so much cash (at least $20B minimum). Explains the conglomerate structure's advantages. |
| [2018ltr.md](parsed/docs/2018ltr.md) | 2018 | PDF | Share repurchases expanded — now authorized whenever price is below intrinsic value. Discusses the "two-column" valuation of Berkshire. Apple described as Berkshire's third major business (after insurance and BNSF). |
| [2019ltr.md](parsed/docs/2019ltr.md) | 2019 | PDF | Japan investment (five trading houses) begins. Discusses the "American miracle" of compounding. Critiques financial engineering and short-termism. |
| [2020ltr.md](parsed/docs/2020ltr.md) | 2020 | PDF | COVID-19 year. Discusses why Berkshire's fortress balance sheet matters in a crisis. Admits mistakes: airline stocks purchased and sold at a loss, Precision Castparts goodwill write-down. |
| [2021ltr.md](parsed/docs/2021ltr.md) | 2021 | PDF | Discusses Berkshire's 2021 share repurchases ($27B). Introduces "float" growth to $147B. Discusses the importance of reinvesting in existing businesses vs. acquisitions. |
| [2022ltr.md](parsed/docs/2022ltr.md) | 2022 | PDF | Discusses Berkshire's Occidental Petroleum stake. Major Apple position commentary. Reiterates "never bet against America" thesis. BNSF and BHE as the two pillars of non-insurance earnings. |
| [2023ltr.md](parsed/docs/2023ltr.md) | 2023 | PDF | Greg Abel formally anointed as successor CEO. Discusses the importance of financial strength and permanent capital. Japan investment expanded. Insurance float at $169B. |
| [2024ltr.md](parsed/docs/2024ltr.md) | 2024 | PDF | Final letter before transition to Greg Abel as CEO (Buffett remains Chairman). Discusses mistakes openly. Pete Liegl (Forest River) tribute. Berkshire paid $26.8B in federal income taxes — a U.S. corporate record. P/C insurance as the "crown jewel." Operating earnings of $47.4B. |
